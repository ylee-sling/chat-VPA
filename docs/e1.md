# E1, lossless drafting on Gemma 4 E2B-it: pre-registration (draft)

**Status, 25 November 2026: a draft. The direction and four decisions are the author's (Section 7). The open
choices of Section 5 and the reading rules of Section 4 read "to be confirmed by the author"; until Section 7
records his confirmation with its date, no number of Step 0 is read by a rule. Step 0's measurements are built
and tested on the CPU with tiny models (Section 8); nothing has run on the server, and nothing is trained in
Step 0. Step 1 is a sketch (Section 6) and is registered in a later revision of this document, before any
training.**

E1 is the first experiment after E0 was closed with a negative result (`docs/e0.md`, Section 10: the Block-DFM
realizer on the frozen Gemma 4 E2B cannot decode alone at B = 16; Pass@1 0.4% [0.0, 1.1] at (16, 4), *measured*).
It leaves the preprint's protocol (Sec. 6.2 and after), by the author's decision of 25 November 2026: a change of
product, benchmark and method that the preprint does not describe. Where this document and the preprint
disagree, this document holds for E1, and the preprint is not edited unasked
(`docs/preprint-corrections.md` lists what its next revision has to take up).

How numbers are labelled here: *measured* (by which run, on which device, runtime and precision), *reported* (by
which source), *computed* (arithmetic, no model involved) or *predicted*. A cell printed as `--` is filled by
this protocol.

## 1. The question, and what is already decided

**The question.** On the released `google/gemma-4-E2B-it`, frozen: can a drafter whose tokens the model itself
verifies produce *the model's own text* faster than (a) the model decoding alone and (b) the drafter Google
ships for this model, on the machines and in the runtime of a local product?

Under exact verification the output is the frozen model's, so there is no quality to trade: the results of E1 are
**the exact-match rate, the accepted tokens per cycle and the wall-clock speed**, each named with its device,
runtime and precision. Benchmark scores (IFEval), repetition and perplexity are checks of exactness and
workloads, not results (decision 1).

**What changed the design.** Google released Multi-Token Prediction drafters for the Gemma 4 family on
5 May 2026 under Apache 2.0 (`google/gemma-4-E2B-it-assistant` for this model). All of the following is
*reported* (Gemma 4 Technical Report, arXiv:2607.02770, Sec. 2.6; "Efficient and Robust Drafting with Multi-Token
Prediction", arXiv:2611.25813; LiteRT-LM's Gemma 4 page; the sources are listed in the project note
`claude/e1-lossless-drafting-notes.md`), none of it measured by us:

| | |
|---|---|
| The drafter | one 4-layer head (3 local layers, 1 global), width 256 and 76M parameters for E2B, applied autoregressively; its input is the target's last-layer activation with the token embedding; it attends to the target's KV cache and keeps none of its own; a clustered output layer (2,048 clusters of 128 tokens, exact logits for the top 32 clusters) |
| Speed, E2B | 1.97x in vLLM on an H100 (bf16, batch 1: 278.8 to 550.1 tok/s; block efficiency 2.71 at draft length 4); in LiteRT-LM up to 2.2x on mobile GPUs and up to 1.5x on mobile CPUs, and for E2B on a CPU "a possible slight slowdown" on free-form text |
| An independent measurement (github.com/iprajax/gemma4-mtp, MacBook M4 Pro) | 1.71x in LiteRT-LM on the GPU (84.2 to 144.3 tok/s) but 1.04x in Hugging Face Transformers (21.9 to 22.9 tok/s); outputs diverged at longer lengths in bf16 |
| Adaptation | fine-tuning the target alone lowers the drafter's block efficiency by 21 to 36% (2.51 to 1.60 and 3.73 to 2.95 on two tasks); distilling the drafter again restores most of it |

So the author's Option A (a top-layer drafter with exact verification) exists first-party for this backbone, and
its reported gain depends on the runtime. E1 is therefore defined against it, and starts by measuring it here.

**Decided by the author on 25 November 2026** (Section 7 has his words):

1. E1 is defined against Google's drafter and starts with Step 0: baselines without training, the ceiling, and
   the E0 bridge. The contributions it aims at are the ceiling analysis for local machines, one-shot dependent
   block drafting (arm A3), adaptation to Korean and public-sector text, and the bridge from E0 to verified
   decoding.
2. The downloads: `google/gemma-4-E2B-it`, Google's drafter, the IFEval checker, UltraChat-200K (for research and
   prototyping; its licence is audited before any commercial distribution).
3. Further runtimes on the server, LiteRT-LM and vLLM, in environments of their own.
4. "Local PC" means enterprise and public-sector laptops with an integrated GPU (Intel Core Ultra, AMD Ryzen AI,
   Apple M-series). On the server, Step 0 measures the L4 (a discrete-GPU upper bound, fast to iterate on) and
   the CPU with a fixed number of threads (a CPU-only lower bound). Before any product claim the same LiteRT-LM
   measurement runs on a physical laptop of that kind.

## 2. Before any measurement: setup and preflight (`scripts/ncp/e1_setup.sh`)

One command runs three steps in tmux; each can be run again and skips what is complete.

| Step | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `assets` | downloads the target, Google's drafter, LiteRT-LM's build of the model (`litert-community/gemma-4-E2B-it-litert-lm`, drafter inside), UltraChat-200K's `test_sft` split and a sparse checkout of the IFEval checker; writes the development prompts | `runs/assets/` |
| `runtimes` | creates three environments: vLLM, LiteRT-LM, and the IFEval checker's packages | `runs/assets/venvs/` |
| `preflight` | prints what the author pastes back: the machine; the assets; the target's and the drafter's architecture and parameter counts; the chat prompt as rendered (thinking must be off); a smoke decode with and without the drafter; vLLM's version and its speculative-decoding option; LiteRT-LM's version, interface and what the model file says about itself; the IFEval checker's self-test | `runs/assets/preflight.log` |

The server's rules are relaxed here and only here, by decision 3: the script uses the network; it creates
environments besides text-VPA's `.venv`, inside `runs/`, so that no other torch reaches the training environment;
it adds `pyarrow` to text-VPA's `.venv` if that is missing (it reads the parquet files; it pins nothing). Foreign
runtimes run with their home directory under `runs/assets/home`, so that what they cache stays inside `runs/`.
Nothing is written outside `runs/`, and nothing shuts the server down.

A line of the preflight that starts with `UNEXPECTED` is a failed expectation; Step 0 does not start before each
is understood. The smoke decode is a check that the pieces fit, not a result.

**The IFEval checker is not repeatable as shipped** (*measured* in the development sandbox, google-research
`20fadc5`, on the GPT-4 responses the repository ships). Its language checks use `langdetect`, which is
randomised, and `instructions.py` draws from Python's `random` module in 24 places. With only
`langdetect.DetectorFactory.seed = 0` set, three runs on the same responses gave strict prompt-level accuracy
0.7722, 0.7704, 0.7704 and loose 0.7981, 0.7944, 0.7981: a prompt or two of 540 changed sides. With
`random.seed(0)` set as well, three runs in one environment and two in a second gave the same four numbers:
strict 0.7722 / 0.8377, loose 0.7981 / 0.8570 (prompt-level / instruction-level, on the 540 of 541 prompts that
the shipped responses cover; the paper, arXiv:2311.07911, reports 76.89 / 83.57 and 79.30 / 85.37 for GPT-4).
The preflight repeats this self-test on the server. Consequence: C9 below.

## 3. Step 0: what is measured (nothing is trained)

The target is always the released checkpoint, frozen. A response is one user turn in the model's own chat
template with the generation prompt, no system prompt, thinking off. Decoding is greedy in every arm.

**Development prompts** (`chat_vpa/e1_prompts.py`): the first 200 first user turns of UltraChat-200K's
`test_sft` split that have at least one non-blank character and at most 4,000 characters, in file order; nothing
is drawn at random. `train_sft`, from which Step 1 would take its training prompts, is disjoint from it.
Everything that is tuned in E1 is tuned on this file. IFEval is not read in Step 0.

### 3.1 Baselines in Hugging Face Transformers (`chat_vpa/e1_decode.py`)

All arms go through the released model's own `generate`, batch 1, `do_sample=False`:

| Arm | What decodes |
|---|---|
| `ar` | the model alone, one pass per token (the baseline) |
| `mtp:G`, G in {2, 4, 6, 8} | with Google's drafter (`assistant_model=`): G drafted tokens per cycle, one target pass over G + 1 positions keeps the drafted tokens that equal its own argmax and adds its next token |
| `lookup:10` | prompt-lookup decoding: drafts copied from the context, no drafter model (what repetition alone is worth) |

Recorded per prompt and arm: the new tokens, the wall-clock time of the call, the time until the first new tokens
arrive, and the forward passes of the target and of the drafter (counted with hooks, as `PassAudit` counts).
Reported per arm: decode tok/s (tokens after the first arrival over the time after it), end-to-end tok/s,
tokens per cycle, the exact share (the arm's tokens equal `ar`'s for the same prompt in the same run, with the
position of the first difference otherwise), and the speed-up over `ar` with a 95% paired bootstrap over prompts.

| Run | Device, precision | Prompts x new tokens | Arms |
|---|---|---|---|
| `decode/gpu-bf16` | L4, bfloat16 | first 100 x 256 | all six |
| `decode/gpu-bf16/profile` | L4, bfloat16, timers around every pass | first 10 x 256 | `ar`, `mtp:4` (where the time goes; never used for a speed-up) |
| `decode/gpu-fp32` | L4, float32 (may not fit 24 GB: then it is reported as not run) | first 50 x 256 | `ar`, `mtp:4` |
| `decode/cpu-fp32` | CPU, float32, 4 threads | first 20 x 128 | `ar`, `mtp:2`, `mtp:4` |

### 3.2 The ceiling (`chat_vpa/e1_ceiling.py`)

Speculative decoding replaces n target passes over one token by one pass over n tokens; it can only pay where
that pass is cheaper than the n it replaces. On the target alone, for the first 20 prompts (5 on the CPU): the
prompt is prefilled, 32 tokens are decoded one pass at a time, and one pass over the first n of them is timed
from the same prompt cache, for n in {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32}.

    t(n)         median seconds of a pass over n new positions (measured)
    ceiling(n)   = n t(1) / t(n): the speed-up of a drafter that costs nothing and is always right (predicted)
    P(n, alpha)  = E t(1) / t(n), E = (1 - alpha^n) / (1 - alpha): the speed-up of a free drafter whose tokens
                 are accepted independently with probability alpha, alpha in {0.5, ..., 0.9} (predicted)

The same passes answer the numerical question behind "lossless": at how many positions does a pass over n tokens
choose another token than n passes over one token (`argmax differs`)? In exact arithmetic, none. Runs: L4 in
bfloat16, L4 in float32 if it fits, CPU in float32 with 4 threads.

t(1) is a bare pass of the model; `generate` adds its own work per token to every arm of 3.1, which the profiled
run shows.

### 3.3 The E0 bridge (`chat_vpa/e1_bridge.py`)

Orthrus (arXiv:2605.12825) and Uno (arXiv:2609.04010) use E0's construction, a trainable diffusion view that
reads the frozen model's KV cache, as a *drafter* that the frozen model verifies. The bridge measures what E0's
two checkpoints (`runs/e0-T16`, `runs/e0-R16`) are worth in that role as they are, on E0's own evaluation prompts
(MBPP validation, 90 problems, the base checkpoint they were trained on):

1. y is the frozen backbone's greedy continuation by its own `generate` (up to `<eos>`, the truncation rule's
   stop, or 256 tokens).
2. Along y: at position s the drafter drafts B = 16 tokens after (prompt, y[:s]) with K passes of the block
   sampler at temperature 0; a is the number of leading draft tokens equal to y; the cycle yields a + 1 tokens
   (capped at the end of y) and costs K draft passes and 1 verifying pass:

       tokens per backbone pass = tokens / (cycles (K + 1));        autoregressive decoding = 1.

   With nothing ever accepted this is 1 / (K + 1): 0.5, 0.33 and 0.2 at K = 1, 2, 4 (*computed*).
3. The same drafts at training's block boundaries (s = 0, 16, 32, ...): accepted tokens per block, and the
   agreement with y per slot.

K in {1, 2, 4}, prefix-growing and confidence orders. K = 1 uses a step that E0 did not train. Passes are
counted, not timed. A check first runs the committed stream itself as the drafter on 10 problems (every draft
must be accepted in exact arithmetic; a rejection is a numerical difference between `generate` and this
repository's path in bfloat16, the precision E0 ran in).

### 3.4 Other runtimes

A speed-up measured in one runtime is not a speed-up in another: the cost of a pass is a different mix of
arithmetic and overhead in each. Each runtime is its own table.

**LiteRT-LM** (`chat_vpa/e1_litert.py`; built). Google's on-device runtime with its own build of the model (its
quantisation; the drafter is inside the file): the runtime the product would ship in, and the one in which the
drafter's gain is reported. Modes `off`, `on` and `adaptive` (the runtime speculates only while it measures that
to be faster), lenience 0 (the lossless setting) passed explicitly, the sampler at top-k 1, thinking off, the
first 50 development prompts x 256 tokens, the modes back to back per prompt. Reported: decode tok/s by the
runtime's own timer, end-to-end tok/s by the wall clock, the speed-up over `off` with a paired bootstrap, and how
many responses have the same text as `off`'s. Backends: the CPU with 4 threads, and the GPU if the runtime can
use it (on Linux its GPU backend needs a Vulkan driver; if the L4 offers none, the GPU row is reported as not
run). The texts need not equal 3.1's (another build of the weights): exactness is read inside one runtime.

**vLLM** (not built). The runtime of Google's reported 1.97x; a server engine, not a local one, so it bounds the
L4 from above and is not the product's number. Its arm (`--speculative-config` with method `mtp`) is written
after the preflight has shown the installed version and its option, and is added here as a revision.

### 3.5 How time is measured, in every runtime

- Batch 1. One GPU job at a time; the summary names the device, runtime, precision and thread count.
- The first prompts are decoded and thrown away (2; 1 on the CPU), by every arm.
- The arms of a prompt run back to back, so that a drift of the machine falls on all of them.
- The device is synchronised before a clock is read (PyTorch); LiteRT-LM's decode rate is its own record.
- Decode speed leaves out the time to the first token; end-to-end speed includes prompt processing. Both are
  ratios of sums over prompts, tokens over seconds; a speed-up is the ratio of two such rates.
- Intervals: 95% percentile, paired bootstrap over prompts, 10,000 resamples, seed 0 (`e0_report.paired_bootstrap`).
- The same prompts, token budget and stopping rule for every arm of a table.
- A profiled run is never used for a speed-up.

### 3.6 Cost

*Predicted* from the token counts and a guess of the decode rates, not measured: the bridge takes minutes; the
GPU decode run about 1.5 to 2 hours (100 prompts x 256 tokens x 6 arms at roughly 25 tok/s); the CPU runs and
LiteRT-LM about an hour together. `scripts/ncp/e1_step0.sh` runs the parts one after another in tmux, skips what
has finished, and goes on when a run that may not be possible on the machine fails (float32 on the L4,
LiteRT-LM's GPU backend).

## 4. Reading rules of Step 0 (to be confirmed by the author)

"Clearly" means, as in E0, that the 95% paired-bootstrap interval excludes the value compared with.

**R1, exactness.** For each table (runtime, device, precision): an arm is *exact on this sample* if every one of
its responses equals the reference decoding of the same table (`ar`, or `off`) token for token (LiteRT-LM:
character for character); otherwise it is *not token-identical in this precision*, with the count, the first
differing positions and, for PyTorch, the ceiling's `argmax differs` counts in the same precision beside it. No
speed-up is called lossless from a configuration that is not exact on its sample, and "lossless" is always said
with the runtime and precision.

**R2, whether Google's drafter pays.** For each table, the drafter's best setting is the draft length (or mode)
with the largest decode speed-up; all settings are reported. It *pays clearly* if its interval lies above 1,
*costs clearly* if it lies below 1, and is *undecided* otherwise.

**R3, whether any drafter could pay on a device** (PyTorch, where the ceiling is measured). If ceiling(n) is
below 1.2 for every n up to 8 on a device, speculative decoding cannot gain 20% there whatever the drafter: no
drafter is trained or evaluated for speed on that device in that runtime, and the ceiling curve is the finding.

**R4, which lever is short** (PyTorch, at the drafter's best draft length G, in the precision of the main table).
With tau the measured tokens per cycle:

    acceptance factor  a = tau / (G + 1)                    the share of a cycle's positions that is kept
    cost factor        c = speed-up / (tau t(1) / t(G + 1))  the share of a free drafter's gain that survives
                                                             the drafter's own time and the runtime's overhead

Both are reported with the profiled shares. The smaller factor names the first trained arm of Step 1: the cost
factor points to A3 (fewer drafter calls per cycle), the acceptance factor to A2 (a drafter distilled on the
target's own text). This fixes the order before the numbers are seen; it does not register Step 1.

**R5, the bridge.** A row (checkpoint, K, order) *breaks even* if its tokens per backbone pass are at least 1
(clearly: the interval's lower end is at least 1). Arm B1 (Section 6) enters Step 1 only if some row of T16 breaks
even; otherwise it is deferred behind the A arms and the bridge's table is reported as it is. Whatever the
outcome, the bridge is not a statement about dialogue: it is code, on the base checkpoint.

## 5. Open choices: each "to be confirmed by the author"

| | Choice | Recommended default | Alternatives, and why not |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | The prompt | one user turn in the model's chat template, no system prompt, thinking off | thinking on: longer outputs of another kind, and a second workload to describe; a system prompt: a product decision that E1 does not need yet |
| C2 | The decoding rule | greedy in every arm and runtime; exactness is token identity with the same table's reference | the model card's sampling (T = 1.0, top-p 0.95, top-k 64): verification then preserves the distribution, which one sample cannot show; it becomes a second protocol if the product samples |
| C3 | Development prompts | the first 200 qualifying first user turns of UltraChat-200K `test_sft`; 100 for the GPU table, 50 for LiteRT-LM and float32, 20 for the CPU and the ceiling | a random draw (nothing gained, a seed to carry); IFEval's prompts (a test set: agreement 3) |
| C4 | Token budget | 256 new tokens (128 in the CPU table of 3.1) | 512: twice the time for the same quantities; the budget is revisited with the product's typical length |
| C5 | Draft lengths | G in {2, 4, 6, 8}; prompt lookup at 10 | only Google's default: the best length may differ by runtime, and R2 and R4 are read at the best |
| C6 | Devices and precisions on the server | L4 in bfloat16 (main), L4 in float32 if it fits, CPU in float32; LiteRT-LM on the CPU and, if a Vulkan driver exists, the GPU | the L4 only: decision 4 asks for the CPU lower bound |
| C7 | CPU threads | 4, fixed, in both runtimes (the performance cores of a thin laptop; the server's CPU is another CPU, so this is a lower bound of its own kind, not a laptop's number) | all cores: not comparable with a laptop, and LiteRT-LM and PyTorch would differ in what "all" means |
| C8 | The primary speed number | the decode speed-up (after the first token), with the end-to-end speed-up beside it | end-to-end only: it mixes prompt processing, which no drafter changes, into the ratio |
| C9 | IFEval | untouched until a protocol run that a later revision registers; the checker is then run with `random.seed(0)` and `langdetect.DetectorFactory.seed = 0`, and the scores are reported with those seeds | as shipped: two runs of the checker on the same responses differ (Section 2), so an "equal score" check of exactness could fail for a reason that has nothing to do with the decoder |
| C10 | R3's threshold | ceiling(n) below 1.2 for every n up to 8 | 1.0: a strict bound that a 5% gain would pass; 1.5: would rule out devices where a real if small gain exists |
| C11 | R5's threshold for B1 | break-even, 1 token per backbone pass, for some row of T16 | run B1 regardless (one more training run of about E0's cost, with roughly 1/50 of the training tokens Orthrus reports); drop B1 (the bridge is then E0's last word) |

## 6. Step 1: a sketch (registered in a later revision, before any training)

One change per run; every arm is verified by the frozen target and compared in the same table with `ar` and
with A1.

| Arm | Drafter | The one change |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Google's drafter as shipped | the baseline |
| A2 | A1's architecture, distilled on the target's own greedy rollouts for `train_sft` prompts (and, for the product, Korean and public-sector prompts) | the weights |
| A3 | A2 with several dependent positions per head call (the author's one-shot block drafting; cf. SpecBlock, arXiv:2605.07243) | the number of head calls per cycle |
| B1 | E0's Block-DFM with verification: one denoising step, forward KL to the frozen model's distribution (Orthrus's objective) | the drafter is the backbone itself |

Known before it is designed, and not verified by us: LiteRT-LM runs the drafter that is packed into the model
file, so an arm with A1's architecture (A2) could be shipped in it by Google's conversion path, while A3 and B1
need changes to a runtime and are research arms in PyTorch. Whether that conversion path is open to us is a
question for Step 1's design. The protocol run (IFEval as a workload and an exactness check, a Korean set) is
registered with it.

`claude/e1-lossless-drafting-notes.md`):

1. *"Define E1 against Google's official E2B drafter, starting with Step 0 (no-training baseline + ceiling + E0
   bridge): Yes, agreed."* IFEval, repetition and perplexity are checks of exactness and workloads; the results
   are the exact-match rate, the accepted tokens and the wall-clock time.
2. *"Offline downloads to the server (`google/gemma-4-E2B-it`, Google's official E2B drafter, the `IFEval`
   checker, and `UltraChat-200K`): Yes, approved. We will use `UltraChat-200K` for E1 research/prototyping and
   audit dataset licensing before any commercial distribution."*
3. *"Installing additional runtimes (`LiteRT-LM` and `vLLM`): Yes, explicitly approved. Override the
   single-environment rule as needed (using separate isolated venvs/binaries if cleanest)"*.
4. The target hardware that defines "local PC": *"Modern integrated-GPU (iGPU) enterprise/public-sector AI PCs &
   laptops"*; on the server, the L4 and the CPU with fixed threads; *"before any product claim we will run the
   exact same `LiteRT-LM` benchmark on a physical iGPU / M-series laptop."*

**Not yet decided:** C1 to C11 and R1 to R5.

Decisions without the author (agreement 7; routine engineering): everything E1 downloads or installs lies under
`runs/assets/`; foreign runtimes get a home directory there; the LiteRT-LM arm was written before the preflight,
because the package could be installed and its interface read in the development sandbox (Section 8), while the

| `chat_vpa/e1_decode.py` | 3.1 |

| `chat_vpa/e1_bridge.py` | 3.3 |
| `chat_vpa/e1_litert.py` | 3.4, LiteRT-LM (runs in LiteRT-LM's environment; no torch) |
| `chat_vpa/e1_preflight.py`, `e1_report.py` | Section 2's Transformers checks; Step 0's tables as they stand |
| `scripts/ncp/e1_setup.sh`, `e1_step0.sh` | Section 2; Step 0 on the server |
| `tests/test_e1.py`, `tests/test_e1_litert.py` | 16 tests on the CPU: a tiny random Gemma 4 with a tiny random drafter; a stand-in for the LiteRT-LM package |

Tested on the CPU with the tiny models: every verified arm of 3.1 reproduces the model's own greedy `generate` in
float32; a pass over n tokens chooses what n passes choose; the bridge accepts exactly where an explicit loop
accepts, and the committed stream as its own drafter is always accepted in float32. **In bfloat16 it is not**: on
the tiny model `generate` and the realizer's path chose different tokens at 2 of 48 positions (*measured*, CPU).
The same kind of finite-precision disagreement is *reported* for Orthrus (arXiv:2609.15504: greedy trajectories
equal the AR reference on 45% of 1,190 prompts in BF16 and on all in FP32). This is why R1 is read per precision.

Both scripts ran end to end in the development sandbox on the CPU with stand-ins (the tiny models; LiteRT-LM
0.20.0 from PyPI with the 49 MB test model of its repository; the real IFEval checker). **Not tested there, and
therefore first exercised on the server:**

- the downloads from the Hugging Face Hub (not reachable from the sandbox): the repository names, the file name
  of LiteRT-LM's model, and whether the Hub asks for a login;
- the installation of vLLM (its package resolves for Python 3.12 as `vllm` 0.34.0 with its own torch 2.14.0,
  *computed* by the resolver; it was not installed);
- LiteRT-LM's `on` and `adaptive` modes and its GPU backend (the test model has no drafter, and the sandbox has
  no Vulkan driver: both failures were recorded as the script is meant to record them);
- everything on a GPU, and the real checkpoints in any precision.

## 9. What Step 0 cannot show

- **Not the target hardware.** The L4 is a discrete GPU and the server's CPU is not a laptop's; decision 4's
  laptop run is a separate measurement. PyTorch in eager mode is not a product runtime.
- **Not the product's text.** English first turns of UltraChat, single turn, at most 256 tokens; no Korean, no
  public-sector documents, no long context.
- **Greedy only** (C2). Nothing is said about sampled decoding.
- **Exactness on a sample.** "Exact on 100 prompts" is a statement about 100 prompts in one precision.
- **No ceiling in LiteRT-LM.** Its Python interface gives no pass over n positions to time, so R3 and R4 are
  read in PyTorch only, and LiteRT-LM contributes R1 and R2.
- **The bridge is not dialogue**, and its drafters were trained for another purpose with about 20M target tokens
  (*computed*: 10,000 steps x 32 examples x about 63 tokens).
