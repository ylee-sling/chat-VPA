# E0, the bridge experiment: pre-registration

**Status, 23 November 2026: confirmed by the author, C1 to C12 (Section 9), and frozen: a later change is a
deviation. The checks of Section 2 have run (Section 2.4). The rollout writer, the trainer, the evaluation and the
report are built and tested on the CPU (Section 8); the writer has run on the L4 and the training data is written
(Section 4, step 1). No training run has finished yet.**

E0 is the first experiment of the Chat-VPA preprint (draft of 22 November 2026, Section 6.2). This document fixes
how it is run and how its result is read, before it is run. The preprint fixes most of the design; where it is
silent, Section 3 below lists the choice, the alternatives, a recommended default and the reason. **Every choice
in Section 3 is the author's and reads "to be confirmed by the author" until Section 9 records his decision with
its date.** After confirmation this document is frozen: a later change is a deviation, recorded in Section 9 with
its date and reason, and the affected result is labelled.

How numbers are labelled here: *measured* (by which run), *reported* (by which source), *computed* (arithmetic on
the code's own rules, no model involved) or *predicted*. A cell printed as `--` is filled by this protocol.

## 1. The question, and what the preprint already fixes

The predecessor's failures were measured on Python (MBPP), and Chat-VPA is designed for dialogue. Before any claim
about dialogue, E0 asks whether block-causal generation and a low-entropy target repair the measured failure in
the setting where it was measured.

## 2. Before the run: three checks on the L4

None of them trains anything. They run before this document is confirmed, because two of the open choices need
their numbers (how many rollouts are affordable; whether the port is exact). One command starts all three in one
tmux session, one after the other: `bash scripts/ncp/first_checks.sh`; `bash scripts/ncp/first_checks.sh show`
prints what to paste back.

### 2.1 Port check (`scripts/ncp/port_check.sh`): does the ported code reproduce the predecessor?

With text-VPA's checkpoint (`~/text-VPA/runs/stage0-a/last.pt`), its settings and its seeds, on MBPP validation
90 x 5, compared sample by sample with text-VPA's own sweep (`~/text-VPA/runs/stage0-a/sweep/mbpp/`):

1. the test suite in the server's environment;
2. the whole-canvas sampler at K = 8, exact kernel, against `realizer-K8/samples.jsonl`;
3. the same checkpoint through the Block-DFM path with one block of B = 256, against the same file;
4. the AR baseline, against `ar/samples.jsonl`.

**Reading rule.** Expected: every raw sample identical in 2, 3 and 4 (450 of 450 each). If so, the port is taken as
exact and text-VPA's AR samples are E0's baseline. If not, the agreement rate is reported, the cause is found
before anything is trained, and no E0 number is produced by a path that differs. `compare_samples` also prints any
setting that the two summaries record differently, so that a difference of settings is not read as a difference of
code. On a tiny random model on the CPU, against samples written by text-VPA's own code at `6f97346`, all three
comparisons give 50 of 50 identical samples (*measured in the development sandbox, 23 November 2026; not the real
model*).

### 2.2 Rollout throughput (`scripts/ncp/rollout_throughput.sh`): how many rollouts are affordable?

256 distinct corpus prompts, batch sizes 16, 32, 64 and 128, the baseline's draw. It writes
`runs/e0-throughput/throughput.json` (rollouts per second, peak memory, how each rollout stopped, the share kept,
program lengths, projected hours for 100,000 and 320,000 prompts and kept rollouts) and `examples.txt` (20 prompts
with reference and rollout). It also reports the entropy rate of the decoding distribution along the rollouts
(Corollary 1's h-bar_T) and the backbone's NLL of rollouts and of the human references of the same prompts. It
decides choice C6.

### 2.3 Block machinery on the real weights (`scripts/ncp/block_check.sh`): a diagnostic of the code

text-VPA's Stage 0 adapters inside block windows, first 10 validation problems x 5 samples, at (B, K) = (16, 16)
prefix-growing (one token per pass inside each block) and (16, 4) exact kernel. Those adapters were trained on
whole canvases and never saw a window that ends after B slots. **Pass@1 of this check is not a result and is not
reported as one.** What it is for, with the rules written now:

- **Pass audit.** Every block must cost exactly its K-bar passes, with the commit rows leaving before the first
  KV-sharing block (Proposition 2). The audit raises otherwise; the summary must say `passed`.
- **Cache spot check** (3 problems). The cache built by the fused commits is compared with the frozen backbone's
  one-pass prefill of the same committed tokens, next to two yardsticks measured in the same run: the cache built
  by separate commit passes (what building a cache block by block costs in bfloat16 rounding) and the committed
  tokens read with the adapters acting on them (what a leak would look like). Rule: *consistent with rounding* if
  the fused commit's largest difference is at most 4 times the separate commit's; *uninformative* if the leak
  yardstick is less than 10 times the rounding yardstick; otherwise *differs beyond rounding*, and that is
  investigated before anything else. (In float32 the fused, the separate and the one-pass cache agree to 1e-5 on a
  tiny random model; that is a unit test.)
- **Seconds per block and per pass**, with the batch size they were measured at: the evaluation cost of Section 4.

Summary; the paragraphs below give alternatives and reasons.

| C8 | Sampler in evaluation | no early exit (delta = 0); fused commit; the baseline's draw; at most ceil(256 / B) blocks; seed 0 | confirmed, 23 Nov 2026 |
| C9 | Decisions of the implementation | as recorded in `CLAUDE.md`, listed below | confirmed, 23 Nov 2026 |
| C10 | Decision rule of H2 | sign at each cell; ordering of the differences over g; interval of D(32, 2) - D(16, 4) | confirmed, 23 Nov 2026 |
| C11 | Reading of a failed H1 | the table of Section 5.4 | confirmed, 23 Nov 2026 |

**C1. Loss.** *Default:* the masked cross-entropy L_DFM of Eq. 12 on each arm's own target, at masked slots, with
omega = 1 + lambda_seam = 2 on the first b0 = 4 slots of every block, 0.1 on the `<eos>` filling after the first
`<eos>`, and 1 elsewhere (the preprint's proposed values). No L_Block-KD, L_Boundary, L_Step or L_Plan; L_AR has
nothing to train, because the clean stream is the frozen backbone. *Reason:* Section 6.2 says the arms differ in
the target only. The teacher's stored distributions exist for rollouts and not for human references
(Proposition 5 is about trajectories drawn from the teacher), so a soft-target term would be a second difference
between the arms. *Alternatives:* (a) lambda_seam = 0, which is the predecessor's weighting; (b) a third arm
later, T + L_Block-KD on right-open slots, as one change from arm T: it needs the top-k distributions stored with
the rollouts, which the default does not store.

**C2. Training budgets.** *Default:* each block of each example draws K uniformly from {2, 4}, then t = k/K
uniformly on that grid, then a prefix-shaped state with probability rho_pre = 0.5 and independent reveals
otherwise (Section 3.3). *Reason:* E0 has no step distillation, so the model must be trained at the budgets it is
evaluated at; Table 2's stage D0 trains at {8, 6} only because D2 and D3 halve them. With these budgets the
prefix-shaped training states are exactly the states that the prefix-growing sampler visits (*computed, and a
unit test*): 0, 2, 5, 10 revealed slots at (16, 4); 0, 5 at (16, 2); 0, 3, 10, 20 at (32, 4); 0, 10 at (32, 2).
*Alternatives:* (a) one model per K; (b) a third budget K = B, which would add a one-token-per-pass row inside
blocks, the counterpart of the predecessor's 17.1%, and would separate "block windows cost something" from
"few passes cost something" if H1 fails, at the price of a third of the training states. Not in the default,
because it is a second question in the same run.

**C3. Block size in training.** The network is never told B; it sees it as the extent of its window. *Default:*
one model per arm and block size, four runs. *Reason:* H1 is read on one cell, (16, 4). If one set of adapters
also had to serve B = 32 and H1 failed, the mixture would be a second candidate cause. *Alternative:* one model per
arm, B drawn per micro-batch from {16, 32} (four micro-batches per step, so every step sees both): two runs, half
the GPU time, half the training states per block size. *Cost:* at the speed of `runs/stage0-a` (*measured:*
10,000 steps in 13.8 h, 0.202 steps/s, whole canvases of 257 rows) four runs would take 55 h and two runs 28 h.
**That speed is not a measurement of the block trainer**, whose gradient pass has M (B + 1) rows for a response of
M blocks instead of 257; the first 100 steps of the first run are the timing measurement (Section 4).

**C4. Registers.** *Default:* R = 0. *Reason:* the preprint's default of R = 2 is the student's; Section 6.2 does
not name R for E0, and Section 3.4 predicts no effect on the factorization term. Registers would add trained
vectors that the predecessor's recipe does not have, and H5 (R = 2 against R = 0) is a hypothesis about the
student. *Alternative:* R = 2 in both arms, 19 instead of 17 rows per window at B = 16.

**C5. Initialization.** *Default:* fresh adapters in all runs (LoRA at its initial values, zero modulation, the
mask vector at the `<mask>` embedding), the starting point of `runs/stage0-a` itself. *Reason:* `stage0-a/last.pt`
carries 10,000 steps on human references; starting arm T from it would put the reference target into the rollout
arm. *Alternative:* both arms from `stage0-a/last.pt`: faster, and H2 would then compare 10,000 further steps on
each target rather than the targets themselves.

**C6. Data.** *Default as first proposed* (superseded by the decision at the end of this paragraph):
- *Prompts.* The examples that `CorpusStream` yields with the seed and settings of `runs/stage0-a` (canvas 256,
  prompts of at most 512 tokens, `write_fraction` 0.5, which gives a `write` share of about 14%: Section 2.4), in
  that order. A prompt enters **both** arms or neither.
- *Rollouts.* One per prompt, from the frozen backbone through the released model's own `generate`, with the
  baseline's draw (0.2, top-k 1,024, top-p 0.95), at most 256 new tokens, a fixed seed. The text is cut by the
  shared truncation rule, tokenised again and followed by one `<eos>`. Tokens only are stored.
- *Drop rule.* A rollout that reaches 256 new tokens without `<eos>` and without the truncation rule finding the
  program's end, or whose program does not fit the canvas, is dropped, and its prompt leaves both arms.
- *No quality filter.* Rollouts are not filtered by whether they parse, run or resemble the reference: the bound
  is about the teacher's distribution under its decoding rule, and a filter would change that distribution. A
  rollout whose program is empty is kept; the throughput check reports how often that happens, before this
  choice is confirmed.
- *Number.* N_r = 320,000 kept prompts, which is 10,000 steps of 32 examples with no example seen twice, as in
  `runs/stage0-a`; with four runs under C3 the same 320,000 serve all four.
*Alternative:* N_r = 100,000, each seen 3.2 times in both arms: less rollout time, and a difference from the
predecessor's recipe (repeated examples). *Cost:* at the measured rate and kept share of batch 128, 35.2 h for
320,000 kept rollouts and 11.0 h for 100,000 (Section 2.4).

*What the throughput check found* (Section 2.4), and why C6 waited for an inspection:
- Under the drop rule the `write` format nearly vanishes from the data: 94.4% of `write` rollouts reach the
  budget, and 2 of the 145 kept rollouts are `write` (1.4%), against 14% `write` prompts in the stream and the
  `write` format of every MBPP prompt that E0 is evaluated on. A prompt whose rollout is dropped leaves both arms,
  so the reference arm would lose them too.
- Why, as far as the code says: the `write` rule (`postprocess.py`) stops only at a line that starts with
  `assert `, `print(`, `if __name__`, `"""`, `'''`, a code fence or `<`; a new top-level `def` does not stop it,
  because an MBPP program may define helpers. A `write` prompt is a module docstring, and a rollout that goes on
  writing its module after the function runs to the budget. On MBPP's own prompts the same rule fires: the AR
  baseline stopped after about 120 tokens on average (sequential depth 120, *measured on the predecessor*, text-VPA
  `docs/stage0.md`), where those prompts end with a real test assert.
- Inspected (Section 2.4): the stop rule is not the main cause. In 37 of 64 `write` rollouts the named function
  is never defined, and in 16 more it is still open at the budget. Cutting at the end of the named function (the
  candidate considered here before the inspection) would turn 5 of the 55 rollouts at the budget into programs,
  leave `write` at about 5% of the kept data, and add a training-only truncation rule unlike the evaluation's.

*Decision, confirmed by the author on 23 November 2026 after the inspection:*
- **The drop rule as it is, and no content filter.** 3 of the 9 kept `write` rollouts do not define the named
  function. They stay: filtering them would give arm T a better teacher than the backbone on the evaluation's own
  format. The share of kept `write` targets that define the named function is reported.
- **The kept set stratified by format at the stream's `write` share, 13.5%.** One quota per format, each filled
  in stream order, with every prompt in both arms; the training order interleaves the two formats at those shares.
  Without it, `write` would be between 1.4% (measured, 2 of 145) and about 3% (at the `write`-only run's kept
  rate) of both arms' data, while every MBPP prompt is `write` and `runs/stage0-a` trained on about 13.5% `write`:
  a failed H1 could then be format coverage rather than the method. The price is a selection: the kept `write`
  prompts are those whose rollout ends within the budget, in both arms alike.
- **N_r = 100,000.** A stratified set needs N_r / (`write` kept rate) stream examples to fill its `write` quota.
  At a kept rate between 14.1% (9 of 64) and 11% (11 of 100 draws over both runs), 320,000 would need 2.3 to 2.9
  million, more than the corpus's 1.99 million training functions, so the stream would wrap and repeat `write`
  prompts; 100,000 needs 0.7 to 0.9 million, within one pass. With C7 unchanged each kept prompt is then seen 3.2
  times in 10,000 steps of 32, where `runs/stage0-a` saw each once, the same in both arms. H2 is read together
  with the held-out loss curves of both arms (C7): if arm R's rises while arm T's does not, the repetition favours
  arm T, and the reading of H2 says so.
- *Cost:* about 15 to 17 h (*predicted* from the measured rates: 133,000 `complete` prompts at 4.46 per second
  and 96,000 to 123,000 `write` prompts at about 4.0 per second, the batch-64 rate of `write` prompts scaled to
  batch 128 as the mixed rates scale); 12 to 21 h within the 95% interval of the `write` kept rate (7.6% to 24.6%).
- *Alternative, not chosen:* N_r = 320,000 without stratification, a single pass as in `runs/stage0-a`, 35.2 h,
  with `write` at 1.4 to 3% of both arms' data.
- *How it is built* (`chat_vpa/rollout_writer.py`, implementation details where the decision is silent): the
  validation set of C7 the same way, the first 256 kept examples of the corpus's validation file at the same
  shares (35 `write`, 221 `complete`); every rollout of a prompt is drawn once, in chunks of candidates sorted by
  prompt length and batches seeded from (seed, chunk, batch); the training order is a seeded permutation of the
  kept set per epoch, the same for both arms and both block sizes. A short check of batch 256 against 128 comes
  first (`bash scripts/ncp/e0_data.sh check`); the batch size changes which draws are made, not their
  distribution, and is recorded per chunk.

**C7. Optimization.** *Default:* AdamW (0.9, 0.999), no weight decay, learning rate 2e-4 with 500 warm-up steps
and cosine decay towards 2e-5 with a horizon of 25,000 steps (so 1.41e-4 at the last step, *computed*), gradient
clipping at 1.0, bfloat16 autocast, 32 examples per step (8 x 4, halved and doubled by the memory check if a
worst-case micro-batch does not fit), seed 0, 10,000 steps: `runs/stage0-a`'s settings. Held-out loss every 2,500
steps on 256 functions of the corpus's validation file, for both arms on both targets (the references and the
rollouts of those 256 prompts). **The final checkpoint is the one evaluated**; no checkpoint is chosen on MBPP.
*Alternative:* a horizon of 10,000 steps for the decay.

**C8. Sampler in evaluation.** *Default:* cosine schedule on the grid t = k/K; exact kernel, prefix-growing and
confidence orders; delta = 0, so no early exit in any pre-registered cell; fused commit; tokens drawn with the
baseline's rule and never `[MASK]`, `<pad>` or `<bos>`; a sample stops at the first block that holds `<eos>` or
once the truncation rule has found the program's end, and after at most ceil(256 / B) blocks; seed 0 with
text-VPA's per-sample seeds; every block under the pass audit.

**C9. Decisions of the implementation where the preprint is silent** (made on 23 November 2026 while building the
core; `CLAUDE.md` holds the list):
- *Blocks of a training response.* M = ceil((program tokens + 1) / B): the block that holds the first `<eos>` is
  the last one trained, because the sampler stops there. Alternative: fill to the canvas of 256 as the predecessor
  did, which trains mostly on `<eos>` filling.
- *Weight of a slot that is both among the first b0 and `<eos>` filling:* the filling's 0.1.
- *Scheduled count of the prefix and confidence orders.* After the pass at t the block has
  ceil(kappa_{t+h} B) revealed slots, at least one more than before, and all after the last pass. Alternative: the
  whole-canvas sampler's rule, round(pi x masked), which at (16, 4) starts the second pass from 1 revealed slot
  where training has 2 (*computed*).
- *Local blocks inside a window:* the two-sided sliding-window rule of the Stage 0 realizer, which never binds for
  B + 1 <= 512 and makes one block of B = N the whole-canvas realizer.

**C10. Decision rule of H2.** The preprint says the difference "grows from g = 4 to g = 8 to g = 16" and does not
say how that is tested; two cells have g = 8. *Proposed:* with D(B, K) = Pass@1 of arm T minus Pass@1 of arm R,
exact kernel, in points, and its 95% interval paired over problems: (i) *exceeds* holds at a cell if D > 0 and is
clear if the interval excludes 0; (ii) *grows* holds if D(16, 4) <= D_8 <= D(32, 2), where D_8 is the mean of
D(16, 2) and D(32, 4), and is clear if the paired interval of D(32, 2) - D(16, 4) excludes 0; (iii) H2 holds if
(i) holds at all four cells and (ii) holds. *Caveat to read with it:* a difference in points shrinks when both arms
approach zero, so a failed (ii) with arm R at the floor everywhere is reported as "not testable at this floor", not
as evidence against Proposition 4.

**C11. Reading of a failed H1.** Section 6.4 uses "near the floor" and "a clear gain" without numbers. *Proposed*
in Section 5.4.

**C12. Order.** T16 and R16 first, so that H1 and the B = 16 half of H2 can be read after two runs; then T32 and
R32. The rules of Section 5 are fixed before any of them, so reading early selects nothing.

## 5. Reading rules

Pass@1 is the mean over the 90 problems of the share of each problem's 5 samples that pass. Ratios and
differences use `chat_vpa/report.py`'s paired bootstrap over problems (10,000 resamples, seed 0): both quantities
are computed on the same resampled problems. The AR baseline is the released model's `generate`, 23.8%
(*measured on the predecessor*; the port check must reproduce its samples).

### 5.1 H1, the bridge (verbatim from Section 6.4)

> In E0, Block-DFM at (B, K) = (16, 4) trained on AR rollouts reaches at least half of the AR baseline's Pass@1
> (23.8%), with the exact kernel. It holds if the ratio is at least 0.5, fails if it is below 0.5, and is called
> clear when the 95% paired-bootstrap interval lies on one side of 0.5. The undistilled whole-canvas reference at
> four to six tokens per pass is 1.6%.

Computed as: Pass@1 of T16 at K = 4, exact kernel, over Pass@1 of the AR baseline. Half of 23.8% is 11.9%.

### 5.2 H2, the target (verbatim)

> At equal (B, K) the arm trained on AR rollouts exceeds the arm trained on human references, and the difference
> grows from g = 4 to g = 8 to g = 16 (Proposition 4, Corollary 1).

Decision rule: C10.

### 5.3 H3, the order (verbatim)

> Prefix-growing reveals are at least as good as the exact kernel at equal (B, K), as Finding 1 suggests. If
> their paired interval includes zero after distillation, we read it as the block structure and the target having
> removed the order effect.

Computed as: Pass@1 with the prefix-growing order minus Pass@1 with the exact kernel, arm T, at each of the four
cells, with its paired interval. "At least as good" holds at a cell if the difference is at least 0. The cell named
in the preprint's Table 5, and the one H3 is decided on, is (16, 4); the other three are reported.

### 5.4 If H1 fails (Section 6.4, verbatim, and the proposed numbers of C11)

> A failure of H1 with the human-reference arm also near the floor would speak against block-causal few-step
> decoding of this backbone at this adaptation budget, and the student would not be trained before the cause is
> found. A failure of H1 with a clear gain of the rollout arm over the reference arm would speak for more
> distillation or fewer tokens per pass.

*Proposed:* "near the floor" is a Pass@1 of arm R at (16, 4) of at most 2.0%, the most any order reached at four
to six tokens per pass on whole canvases (*measured on the predecessor*). "A clear gain" is a paired 95% interval
of D(16, 4) that excludes 0 from above.

| H1 fails and ... | Reading |
|---|---|
| arm R near the floor, no clear gain of arm T | against block-causal few-step decoding of this backbone at this adaptation budget; the student is not trained before the cause is found |
| a clear gain of arm T over arm R | for more distillation or fewer tokens per pass |
| neither | no claim; reported with the loss curves and the outcome tables, and the author decides the next run |

### 5.5 What E0 cannot show

One seed per run: the intervals cover the choice of problems, not the variance between runs. The rollout arm's
teacher is the backbone itself, so its ceiling is the AR baseline. The result is about Python on 450 samples and
about adapters on a frozen backbone, not about the student or about dialogue.

## 8. What is built (after confirmation, before step 1)

Built on 23 November 2026 and tested on the CPU with the tiny Gemma 4 of the test suite; of all this the rollout
writer has run on the L4 (Section 4, step 1):

- **The rollout writer** (`chat_vpa/rollout_writer.py`; `bash scripts/ncp/e0_data.sh check`, then
  `bash scripts/ncp/e0_data.sh`): C6 as decided, the validation set of C7 first. Chunks of 2,048 candidates are
  written as they finish, so a stopped run resumes after its last chunk; a chunk that runs out of memory is redone
  at half the batch size. `summary.json` holds what Section 7 reports of the targets: kept shares and stops by
  format, the share of kept `write` targets that define the named function, program lengths of rollouts and
  references (and how many rollouts are empty programs), and the entropy rate of the first 2,000 kept rollouts of
  each format with the backbone's NLL on both targets.
- **The E0 trainer** (`chat_vpa/train_e0.py`; `bash scripts/ncp/e0_train.sh T16`, and R16, T32, R32): text-VPA's
  Stage 0 loop with `collate_blocks`, `block_training_state` (K in {2, 4}, prefix-shaped states with probability
  0.5) and `dfm_loss` behind its hooks; C7's optimizer and schedule; `timing.json` after the first 100 steps.
  Held-out L_DFM and accuracy on both targets at K = 2 and K = 4: each (target, K) has its own fixed draw of
  states, in batches of 8 whatever the memory check makes of the training micro-batch, so that every run is
  measured on the same states of a target (an implementation detail where C7 is silent). Its checkpoints (stage
  `e0`) hold the registers and the training data's fingerprint, and `chat_vpa/block_evaluate.py` evaluates them.
- **The evaluation** (`bash scripts/ncp/e0_eval.sh T16`, and R16, T32, R32): `chat_vpa/block_evaluate.py` on the
  run's final checkpoint, refused before the run's last step (C7), on MBPP validation, 90 x 5, at the run's B with
  K = 4 and 2 and the three orders of C8, then `chat_vpa/analyze.py`'s outcomes.
- **The report** (`chat_vpa/e0_report.py`; `bash scripts/ncp/e0_eval.sh report`, no GPU): the table of Section 6
  with the intervals of Section 5, H1, H2 by C10 with the loss-curve reading of C6, H3, the reading of Section 5.4
  by C11, the tables of Section 7, and a list of every evaluated configuration that differs from what Section 5
  and C8 fix. A run not yet evaluated is `--` and every rule that needs it pending, so the report can be read
  after T16 and R16 (C12).

*Proposed readings, to be confirmed by the author before any result is read* (two rules use a word that needs a
number this document does not give; the report applies them and says so):
- C10's caveat, "arm R at the floor everywhere": arm R's exact-kernel Pass@1 is at most 2.0%, C11's floor, at all
  four cells.
- C6's "if arm R's [held-out loss] rises while arm T's does not": a run's pooled held-out L_DFM on its own target
  is higher at its last evaluation (step 10,000) than at its lowest one; the rise is printed with it.
- *Open, not applied:* whether H3 gets a floor caveat like C10's, so that both orders at the floor at (16, 4) read
  as "not testable at this floor" rather than as the order effect removed.

Each comes with tests against independent references, as the core did.
